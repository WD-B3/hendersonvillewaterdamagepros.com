Emergency Storm Water Damage in Hendersonville, TN

Storms push rain through roofs, windows and doors. Dispatch is open 24/7, including during and after severe weather.

☎ (629) 205-8280

If a storm has driven rain into your Hendersonville home, move to a safe room, stay clear of downed lines and wet ceilings, and call the 24/7 line when it is safe to. Cover openings from the inside if you can, move belongings away from the wet area, and avoid wet electrical equipment.

Storm water damage cleanup is the removal and drying of water that entered the building through the roof, windows, doors or walls during a weather event. It is different from flood damage, where water rises from the ground. A plain roof leak with no storm event falls under the ceiling page.

When storm damage is an emergency

Storms create two kinds of urgency. One is safety, with downed lines, broken glass and weak ceilings. The other is time, because wet drywall, insulation and flooring begin to deteriorate quickly. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, though during widespread storms demand is high and the dispatcher will be honest about the situation.

The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance publishes guidance after major storms, including tips on filing claims, hiring contractors and avoiding scammers. Be wary of door-to-door repair offers that pressure you to sign right away.

Safety before cleanup

Do not touch downed power lines or anything they touch. If ceilings are sagging or the structure looks damaged, leave and call 911. Never use a generator inside the house or garage.

How storm water gets in

Most storm water enters through a short list of places. Roof damage from wind and impact, including lifted shingles and exposed decking. Broken or poorly sealed windows. Doors and thresholds that wind-driven rain pushes through. Chimneys, vents and skylights. Fallen limbs that open a hole in the roof or wall.

  • Roof openings send water through insulation to the ceiling.
  • Window leaks wet the wall, sill and floor beneath.
  • Door thresholds wet flooring first, and often the subfloor.
  • Gutters and downspouts that fail dump water against walls.
  • Attic vents can let in rain during heavy wind.

What the cleanup includes

  1. Photograph all damage, outside and inside, before cleanup.
  2. Arrange temporary protection for openings, such as tarping or board-up, through a roofer or contractor.
  3. Extract standing water and remove wet insulation and drywall that cannot be dried.
  4. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, using structural drying methods.
  5. Clean contents and remove debris.
  6. Prepare a rebuild scope for roofing, drywall, flooring and paint.

Roofing and window repairs are done by those trades. The restoration crew handles the water inside and can recommend temporary measures to prevent further damage.

Storm history and the practical lesson

The National Weather Service reports that the May 2010 storm system produced an EF1 tornado in Sumner County that caused damage over about 5 miles, alongside historic rainfall across Middle Tennessee. That is regional history, but it shows that rain, wind and flood can arrive together. Storm cleanup often overlaps with flood cleanup when creeks or drains also overflow.

Insurance after a storm

Wind and rain damage is usually handled under a homeowners policy, while rising water is typically excluded unless you hold a flood policy. Policies may set a deadline for notice, so file as soon as you can, photograph everything, and keep receipts for emergency repairs. Calls from ZIP 37075 and 37077 reach the same dispatch, and the crew can supply photos and moisture logs for your adjuster.

Industry standard

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard for professional water damage restoration, and storm or flood water may be classified as contaminated under it depending on where the water came from.

Questions about storm water damage in Hendersonville

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Hendersonville?

Yes. Dispatch is answered 24/7. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, though demand is higher after widespread storms.

Can you tarp my roof?

Temporary roof protection is usually done by a roofer. The dispatcher can tell you how to coordinate it with the interior cleanup.

Is storm water damage covered by insurance?

Wind and rain damage is commonly covered by homeowners policies, and flood damage usually is not. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

How do I avoid storm repair scams?

Be wary of door-to-door offers, verify any contractor license at verify.tn.gov, and do not feel pressured to sign on the spot, as the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance advises.
