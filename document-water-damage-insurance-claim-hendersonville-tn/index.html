How to Document Water Damage for a Claim in Hendersonville, TN

A good claim file is built in the first hour. Here is what to record before anything is moved.

By the Hendersonville Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

The best insurance documentation is made before cleanup starts. Once carpet is pulled and drywall is cut, the original condition cannot be photographed again. If water has damaged your home, stop the source and make the area safe, then take a few minutes to record what you see.

This guide covers what to photograph, what to write down and what to keep. It does not decide what your policy covers. Only your insurer can do that.

Photograph and video everything first

Take wide shots of each affected room, then close-ups of damage, then the source of the water. Video is useful because you can narrate as you walk through. Include ceilings, walls, floors and the contents that got wet.

  • Wide shots of each room from two corners.
  • Close-ups of stains, swollen baseboards and wet carpet.
  • The source, such as a split hose, a corroded fitting or a roof opening.
  • Water on the floor, with a ruler or coin for depth.
  • Serial numbers and labels on damaged appliances and electronics.
  • Anything you throw away, photographed before it goes in the trash.

Write down the facts while they are fresh

Make a simple timeline. Note when you noticed the water, what you did, when you called your insurer and who you spoke with. The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance recommends keeping notes of each conversation, including the person's name and the date, time and content.

List damaged belongings with a description, age and rough value. It is easier to do now than weeks later.

What a restoration crew adds

A restoration crew can supply things homeowners cannot easily produce. Moisture readings from a meter, a thermal camera scan, a sketch showing where the water traveled, and daily drying logs. These show what was wet and when it was dry.

Ask for copies at the end of the job. The same documents help if the claim is reviewed later, and they show the difference between water extraction and the drying stage that follows.

What to keep

Keep the failed part if there is one, such as a burst hose or a corroded fitting. Keep receipts for emergency steps you paid for, such as a plumber or a hotel stay. Keep every estimate, invoice and email. Store copies in a place you can reach even if the house is not livable.

Mistakes that weaken a claim

A few habits make claims harder. Throwing away damaged items before photographing them. Waiting to notify the insurer. Starting major repairs before the insurer has had a chance to see the damage, unless emergency steps are needed. Signing papers you have not read. If in doubt, ask your insurer what it requires.

A simple documentation routine

The easiest way to avoid gaps is to follow the same routine every time. Do it before cleanup starts when it is safe, and repeat it as work moves along.

  1. Take wide photos of each affected room, then close-ups of each damaged item.
  2. Record a short walk-through video and say the date and room aloud.
  3. Photograph the source, such as the failed pipe or appliance, and keep the part if you can.
  4. List damaged belongings with a rough age and value.
  5. Save moisture readings, equipment counts and daily logs from the crew.
  6. Keep every receipt, including hotel stays and meals if you cannot use the home.

What not to throw away

Keep damaged items until your insurer says you may dispose of them, unless they are unsafe or contaminated. If something has to go for health reasons, photograph it first and write down what it was. Store copies of everything in a cloud folder or email them to yourself, since the originals may be water damaged too.

Keep one folder for everything

Claims go smoother when the paperwork lives in one place. Make a single folder, paper or digital, and drop in photos, the adjuster contact, your claim number, estimates, receipts and a running log of calls. When an insurer asks a question three weeks later, the answer takes a minute to find instead of an afternoon.

  • Policy number and the insurer phone number.
  • Claim number and the date you reported the loss.
  • The written scope and any revised versions.
  • Receipts for temporary housing, supplies and repairs.

What to do first, in order

  1. Make the area safe, including shutting off water and power to wet areas where you can.
  2. Photograph and record video before moving anything.
  3. Report the loss to your insurer and write down the claim number.
  4. Start mitigation so the damage does not grow.
  5. Keep damaged items until the adjuster says otherwise.

Professional crews document drying to ANSI/IICRC S500, which is the standard for water damage restoration. Ask for moisture logs and photos in that file.

What to know in Hendersonville

The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance advises policyholders to file as soon as possible, to cooperate and ask which documents and data are needed, and to take photos or video of damage. It also notes that your policy may set a deadline for notice, so read the notice provisions.

If the loss involves flood water, remember that the department says most homeowners and renters policies do not cover flood, and that only a flood policy does. Check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Local questions

How soon should I file a claim after water damage in Tennessee?

As soon as possible. The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance notes that your policy may set a deadline for notice, so read it and call your insurer or agent.

Should I keep damaged items for the adjuster?

Photograph them first, and keep items if the insurer asks to see them. Ask your adjuster before discarding anything large or valuable.

Will the restoration crew talk to my insurer?

The crew can provide photos, moisture logs and a written scope for you to share. You remain the one who files and discusses coverage with your insurer.
