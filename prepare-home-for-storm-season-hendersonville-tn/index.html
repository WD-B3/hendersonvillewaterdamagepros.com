Prepare Your Hendersonville, TN Home for Storm Season

Most storm water damage is easier to prevent than to clean up. Use this checklist before the next round of heavy weather.

By the Hendersonville Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

Preparing for storm season takes a weekend and costs little compared with a flooded room. The goal is to keep water out where you can, to limit what it reaches where you cannot, and to have your paperwork ready if something goes wrong.

This checklist covers the outside, the inside, the paperwork and the plan. It is general advice, and your own home may need more.

Outside the house

Water damage often starts with something outside that was not maintained. Walk the property and look at the points where water enters or should leave.

  1. Clean gutters and make sure downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation.
  2. Look at the roof for lifted shingles, damaged flashing and exposed nail heads.
  3. Check that soil slopes away from the foundation.
  4. Clear window wells and storm drains near the house.
  5. Trim limbs that hang over the roof.
  6. Seal gaps around windows, doors and utility penetrations.

Inside the house

Test the sump pump, if you have one, by pouring water into the pit. Make sure the discharge line is clear and consider a battery backup. Know where the main water shutoff is and that it turns. Check the water heater and appliance hoses. Move valuables off basement floors and into waterproof containers.

If a ceiling or wall already has a stain, find the cause now. A small leak found in dry weather is cheaper than a larger one found during a storm.

Paperwork and insurance

Photograph each room and the exterior, and store the photos where you can reach them from outside the house. Read your homeowners policy to see what it says about water, wind and flood. The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance says most homeowners policies do not cover flood, and that national flood policies usually have a 30-day waiting period, so asking about flood coverage before the storm is the only time it helps.

Make an emergency plan

Keep the dispatch number where everyone can see it. Decide who shuts off the water and power. Keep a flashlight, phone chargers and a first aid kit in a dry place. Know where to go if the house becomes unsafe. If water does enter, storm water damage cleanup explains what happens next, and flood damage cleanup covers rising water.

During a storm, stay away from downed lines and do not enter flooded rooms with live outlets.

A weekend checklist

Most of the work takes an hour or two. Doing it before a forecast saves time when everyone else is rushing.

  1. Clean gutters and make sure downspouts discharge well away from the foundation.
  2. Check the roof from the ground for missing or lifted shingles and look at flashing around vents.
  3. Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit and confirming it starts.
  4. Find the main water shutoff and the electric panel, and make sure everyone in the house knows where they are.
  5. Move valuables off basement floors and store papers in sealed bins.
  6. Take dated photos of each room and the exterior for your records.

What to have on hand

Keep a small kit together in one place. Include a flashlight, batteries, a phone charger, a list of important numbers, a few heavy trash bags, a roll of plastic sheeting and tape. A wet vacuum is useful for small spills, but it does not replace professional extraction for a real flood.

Know your policy before the storm. Ask what is covered, what the deductible is and whether flood needs a separate policy.

After the storm passes

Walk the property when it is safe. Look at the roof line, the gutters, the foundation edge and any low spots where water pooled. Inside, check ceilings and the corners of rooms. Photograph anything you find, then call for help early if you see water, because damage spreads quickly once it starts.

Where to read more

The industry standard for professional water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500. You do not need to read it, but it helps to know that a reputable company can say whether its work follows it.

What to know in Hendersonville

The National Weather Service reports that the May 1 to 3, 2010 flood brought 13.57 inches of rain to Nashville International Airport over two days. It also reports that the Corps of Engineers and the Weather Service coordinated dam releases at least 17 times, and that an EF1 tornado caused damage over about 5 miles in Sumner County. That is regional history, and it is a reason to look up your own address on the FEMA flood map.

Hendersonville borders Old Hickory Lake, a Cumberland River impoundment managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. Ask your insurer about flood coverage and read your policy for notice deadlines.

Local questions

When does storm season start in Middle Tennessee?

The National Weather Service Nashville office publishes seasonal information. Heavy rain, wind and severe storms can occur in more than one season, so preparing is useful year round.

Should I buy flood insurance in Hendersonville?

That is a decision for you and your insurer or agent. The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance notes that flood policies are separate and usually have a 30-day waiting period.

What should I do if a storm damages my roof?

Make the area safe, document damage, cover openings from the inside if possible and call a roofer for a temporary cover. Call the restoration line for water inside.
