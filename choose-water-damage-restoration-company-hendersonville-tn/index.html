How to Choose a Water Damage Company in Hendersonville, TN

You may be choosing a company under stress. This checklist keeps the decision clear.

By the Hendersonville Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

When water is in your house, you may call the first company that answers. That is understandable, but a few questions can protect you from a bad hire. This guide is a buyer checklist, drawn from the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance consumer tips and from the published industry standards.

A good company will answer these questions without hesitation. A pushy one will not.

Questions to ask before you sign

Ask these early. If a company cannot answer them clearly, move on.

  • Is the contractor licensed in Tennessee, and what is the license number? You can verify it at verify.tn.gov.
  • Will the work be done by the contractor's own employees or subcontracted?
  • Do crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage?
  • Can you provide proof of insurance and any required permits?
  • Will you give me a written scope, including what is mitigation and what is rebuild?
  • Will you document moisture readings and give me copies?

Money, deposits and paperwork

The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance says that for home improvement projects, state law limits deposits to one-third of the contract price, and that you should avoid paying in full before the work is complete. It also advises getting at least three references and proof of licenses, permits, insurance and bonding.

Do not sign anything you have not read, and be wary of anyone who pressures you to decide on the spot. A written scope that lists work in phases, such as extraction, drying and rebuild, helps you compare companies.

Red flags

Some signs deserve caution. Door-to-door offers after a storm. Pressure to sign an assignment of benefits or a contract before the scope is explained. Refusal to show a license. Prices quoted over the phone for work no one has seen. A request for full payment up front.

What to expect on a call

A good dispatcher asks about the source, the rooms affected, whether power or gas is involved and whether anyone has shut off the water. They tell you what to do while you wait and do not promise an arrival time they cannot keep. For the types of work involved, see flood damage cleanup and the service area page.

Questions to ask on the first call

A few direct questions tell you a lot. Notice whether the answers are specific or vague.

  • Who will be at the house, and what equipment will they bring?
  • What is included in the scope, and what is not?
  • Will you provide a written scope and price before work begins?
  • How do you document moisture readings and drying progress?
  • Do you work with insurance paperwork, and what do you provide for the file?
  • Who is responsible for permits if the repair requires one?

Red flags worth walking away from

Pressure to sign right away, a request for full payment up front, no written scope and refusal to explain the equipment or process are all warning signs. So is a company that only talks about price and never asks what happened.

Good companies ask about the source, the water type and the time it has been wet. Those answers shape the whole job, and a crew that skips them is guessing.

Comparing two quotes fairly

Put the two scopes side by side and check that they cover the same rooms, the same phases and the same equipment days. A lower number that leaves out drying or disposal is not lower at all.

Putting it together

You do not need to become an expert. You need a short list of questions, a written scope and the willingness to say you will call back. A reputable company will understand if you take a day to compare, as long as the water is stopped and drying has begun. Choose the company that explains the plan clearly and gives you paperwork you can understand.

What to know in Hendersonville

The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance publishes consumer advice on filing claims, hiring contractors and avoiding scammers after storms and freezes. It recommends checking licenses at verify.tn.gov and recording the contractor's license plate and driver's license numbers.

Hendersonville's Building and Codes office at City Hall can tell you which repairs need permits. A contractor who pulls the permit should be able to say so up front.

Local questions

How do I verify a contractor license in Tennessee?

The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance recommends verifying at verify.tn.gov.

How much deposit can a contractor ask for in Tennessee?

The department says state law limits deposits on home improvement projects to one-third of the contract price. Avoid paying in full before the work is complete.

Is a restoration company the same as a contractor?

Often a company does both mitigation and rebuild, but the scopes should be separate in writing so you can compare them with your insurance estimate.
