Musty Smell After Water Damage: Hendersonville, TN Guide

A smell that lingers after a leak is a clue. It usually points to moisture that is still in the house.

By the Hendersonville Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

A musty smell after water damage means something is still damp. Surface cleaning and air fresheners do not fix it, because the odor is coming from materials that have not fully dried. The first job is to find where it comes from.

This guide explains common hiding places, what to do about them and when to call for help. It does not diagnose health effects. Questions about symptoms belong with a physician.

Where the smell hides

Odors travel, but the source is usually in a few predictable places. Carpet pad and subfloor under a room that was wet. The back of drywall and insulation in a wall cavity. The space under cabinets. A crawl space or basement. Stored cardboard and fabric.

  • Lift a corner of carpet and smell the pad.
  • Remove a toe-kick and check under the cabinet.
  • Feel baseboards and the bottom of walls for coolness or dampness.
  • Check closet floors and the back of stored boxes.
  • Look at ceiling spots below upstairs bathrooms.
  • Open the crawl space access and smell, with a flashlight.

Drying is the real deodorizer

Odor control starts with drying. Once the moisture is gone, the source stops producing the smell, and cleaning can finish the job. Crews use moisture meters and thermal cameras to find damp areas that are not visible, followed by air movers and dehumidifiers until readings match a dry reference. See the structural drying page for how this works.

After drying, cleaning removes residue. Deodorizing uses methods such as air scrubbing and sealing, and it works best on materials that are dry.

What to remove versus keep

Materials that stay wet or were soaked by dirty water often hold odor and should be removed. Carpet pad, soaked insulation and crumbling drywall are common examples. Hard materials such as sealed concrete and framing can usually be cleaned and kept.

When the smell is something else

Not every musty smell comes from water. A sewage smell may point to a dry drain trap or a sewer problem, and a sulfur smell may come from a water heater. If the smell is sharp or resembles gas, leave the house and call the gas company. For contamination from a drain, see sewage backup cleanup.

If you think mold is growing, the mold remediation page explains what inspection and testing involve.

Where the smell hides

A musty smell is a clue, and it usually points to moisture somewhere you cannot see. Follow your nose, then check the likely places before assuming it is only stale air.

  • Under carpet and pad, especially along walls.
  • Behind baseboards and inside the bottom of drywall.
  • In wall cavities with old insulation.
  • In cabinet bases and under sinks.
  • Inside HVAC ductwork or around an air handler drain.
  • In a crawl space or under a bathroom floor.

Why air fresheners fail

Fragrance covers the smell for a few hours and does nothing about the cause. If the odor comes back, there is still moisture or damaged material in the structure. A moisture meter and a thermal camera find wet areas faster than guessing.

Do not mix cleaning chemicals trying to beat a smell. Ventilate, dry and call for an inspection if the odor persists for more than a day or two after the area feels dry.

A simple order of operations

Find the moisture, fix the source, dry the materials, remove what cannot be dried and then clean. Skipping ahead to deodorizing is the most common mistake. When the structure is truly dry and the damaged material is gone, the smell usually follows. If it does not, the odor source is still in the building.

What to tell the inspector

Share the timeline, including when the water event happened, what dried and what did not, and where the smell is strongest. Mention any earlier repairs or stains. Those details narrow the search quickly and keep the visit short and focused on the likely wet areas.

Bring photos if you have them, and point out any rooms where the odor gets worse after rain or when the air conditioner runs.

Standards and what to expect

Professionals who deal with mold use ANSI/IICRC S520 as their standard. For a smell without visible growth, the first step is still moisture mapping, and the standard matters more if growth is found.

What to know in Hendersonville

Hendersonville Utility District provides wastewater service for the area. If the smell resembles sewage and affects more than one drain, ask the district whether there is a problem in the public line.

If you plan to rebuild after drying, ask the city Building and Codes office at City Hall about permit needs.

Local questions

Will an air purifier get rid of the smell?

It may reduce odor in the air but does not remove the source. Find and dry the damp material first.

How long does a musty smell last after a leak?

It lasts until the damp materials are dried or removed. That depends on how wet they were and how quickly drying started.

Is the smell in Hendersonville homes caused by the lake?

Not by itself. Smells in a house after water damage usually come from materials inside the house that stayed wet.
