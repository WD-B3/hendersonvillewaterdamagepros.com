What Is Water Mitigation? A Hendersonville, TN Guide

Water mitigation is the set of steps that stop damage from growing. Here is what it includes and what it does not.

By the Hendersonville Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

Water mitigation means limiting the damage after water enters a building. It starts with stopping the source and ends when the structure is dry. It is not the same as repair, which comes afterward and replaces what could not be saved. If you have heard both terms from an insurer or a contractor, this guide explains where the line sits.

The idea is simple. Water does more damage the longer it sits, so mitigation is about speed and sequence. Each step has a job, and skipping a step usually means paying for the same damage twice.

The steps of water mitigation

Mitigation follows a fairly steady order, though every job is different. The crew documents first so the insurance file shows the condition before anything is removed.

  1. Stop the source. This can be a shutoff valve, a repaired pipe or a tarp over a roof opening.
  2. Make the area safe. That means checking power, gas and structural risks.
  3. Document the damage with photos, notes and moisture readings.
  4. Remove standing water with pumps and extraction units.
  5. Remove materials that cannot be saved, such as wet carpet pad or crumbling drywall.
  6. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers until moisture readings match a dry reference.
  7. Clean, treat and deodorize as needed.

Mitigation versus repair versus remediation

The words are used loosely, and it helps to separate them. Insurers and contractors often use them to mean different phases of the same loss.

TermWhat it meansExamples
MitigationLimiting damage right nowWater extraction, tear-out, structural drying
Repair or restorationRebuilding what was removedNew drywall, flooring, paint, trim
RemediationRemoving a specific contaminantMold or sewage cleanup under a containment plan

Why speed matters

Wet drywall and carpet pad begin to break down within a day or two, and wet framing can support mold if it stays damp. The exact timing depends on temperature, humidity and the water source, so there is no single deadline. The practical rule is to start mitigation on the day the water is found.

That is also why the structural drying page explains the equipment and readings in detail. Mitigation that stops at removing the visible water leaves moisture inside materials.

What mitigation does not cover

Mitigation is not a plumbing repair, a roof repair or waterproofing. A plumber fixes the pipe and a roofer fixes the roof, and the restoration crew handles the water. It also does not promise an insurance outcome. Your insurer decides coverage, and you can ask the crew for photos, moisture logs and a written scope to support the claim.

Mitigation versus restoration versus repair

These three words get mixed up, and the mix-up can cost money. Mitigation is the emergency phase that limits further loss. Restoration is the broader process of returning the building to a safe, dry, usable condition. Repair is the finish work, such as new drywall, flooring and paint.

When you read a quote or an insurance letter, check which phase each line belongs to. A company that does only mitigation is not behind schedule when it leaves after drying. The rebuild is simply a separate scope, sometimes with a separate crew.

What mitigation looks like on day one

Most mitigation visits follow the same order. The crew finds and stops the source if it is still running, then checks the extent of the wet area with a moisture meter. Standing water comes out first, because every hour it sits it wicks into more material.

  • Source control, such as closing a valve or tarping an opening.
  • Extraction of standing water with pumps and wet vacuums.
  • Removal of materials that cannot be saved, such as soaked pad or ceiling board.
  • Placement of air movers and dehumidifiers.
  • Photos and readings recorded for the claim file.

Questions to ask about mitigation

Ask what is being removed and why, how many pieces of equipment will run, how often readings will be taken and what number counts as dry. A clear answer to each is a sign the crew is working to a plan and not to a habit.

The short version

Mitigation is the fast, practical work that keeps a bad day from becoming a bad year. Stop the source, remove the water, take out what cannot be saved, dry the rest and keep records. Repair comes after, once the structure is dry and the scope is agreed.

  • Mitigation limits loss. Repair rebuilds.
  • Speed matters most in the first day or two.
  • Readings, not guesses, decide when drying is done.
  • Photos and logs make the claim easier to review.

What to do while you wait for help

  • Stop the source if it is safe to do so.
  • Shut off power to wet areas only when the panel is on dry ground.
  • Lift valuables and electronics off wet floors.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything.
  • Keep children and pets out of contaminated water.

The professional standard for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, which covers classifying water, setting drying goals and recording results.

What to know in Hendersonville

Mitigation often begins with a shutoff. Hendersonville Utility District provides water and wastewater service for the greater Hendersonville area, serving more than 18,000 homes and businesses, so it is the right call for a main line or meter question.

If the repair stage involves rebuilding walls or plumbing, ask the city Building and Codes office at City Hall about permits. Hendersonville has adopted the 2021 International Residential Code and the 2021 International Plumbing Code with amendments, and permit rules vary by the kind of work.

Local questions

Is water mitigation covered by homeowners insurance in Tennessee?

Often, when the damage is sudden and accidental. Policies differ, so check yours and ask your insurer. The Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance advises filing as soon as possible.

Who does water mitigation in Hendersonville?

Restoration companies handle mitigation. This dispatch line matches calls in Hendersonville to licensed and insured professionals and explains the scope and price before work starts.

Can I start mitigation myself?

You can stop the source, shut off power if safe, lift valuables and photograph damage. Extraction and drying equipment is usually best handled by a crew.
